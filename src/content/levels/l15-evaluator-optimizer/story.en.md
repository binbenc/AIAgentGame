The transit warehouse is overloaded and more than 300 smart AC orders are delayed. The support lead has the agent write apology emails in bulk, and the very first batch goes wrong:

- Some emails don't mention the order number, and customers reply asking "which order do you mean?"
- Some only apologize and offer no compensation, which makes customers angrier.
- Worst of all, one says "We guarantee delivery by October 5!" It didn't arrive, and the complaint escalated all the way to **Vera**.

> **Vera (security lead)**: Promising delivery dates to customers is a compliance red line. We can't leave that to luck.

> **Zhou**: When one model is both the author and the reviewer, it has a hard time spotting its own problems. Split it into two roles: a **generator** that writes, and an **evaluator** that goes through a **clear rubric** point by point and gives structured feedback. The generator revises based on the feedback until it passes.

> **Zhou**: But don't let them revise forever. Set a round limit. If it still hasn't passed at the end, deliver the **highest-scoring** version, not the last one: revisions can make things worse, too.
